# NOW -- t27b: compile-time float arithmetic folded in binary128 (2026-10-06)

## comptime_float as Zig folds it (Closes #7021)

- New conformance spec `specs/tri/t27b/conformance/comptime_float_f128.t27`. It gives 10 pass under `t27c test-report` (none vacuous), and the same 10 under t27b. A negative control with three asserts flipped fails those three under the reference.
- Zig's comptime_float is an IEEE binary128 value: a literal rounds to binary128 (nearest, ties to even), `+ - * /` between two of them fold in binary128, and two of them compare exactly. The value is rounded to f64 or f32 only when it meets one. t27b used to keep the f64 value and refused (`ExprBinary(*)` and friends) whenever an operand was not exactly an f64. It now does the same binary128 arithmetic.
- Still refused, each by name: a folded value or literal that overflows f64, a constant division by zero, and `@intFromFloat` of a compile-time float that is not exactly an f64.
- Two f32 midpoint literals that share one f64 (1 + 2^-24 written two ways) now round to the two f32s Zig gives, instead of being refused.
- Code is in `cli/t27b/src/lower/f128.rs` (new), with the uses in `lower.rs` and `lower_float.rs`. Tests are in `cli/t27b/tests/comptimef128.rs` and `cli/t27b/tests/differential.rs`. Rust is edited under the owner's `owner-approved-foreign` approval on #6063; the files are listed in `tools/policy/foreign-exceptions.txt`.
