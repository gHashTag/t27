  No quiet gate names a tracked path that is missing today. That is a
  result, not an absence of one: the shapes are there and the subjects
  are still on disk.

  `grep` exits 2 for "no such file" and 1 for "no match", and those are
  different answers. `2>/dev/null` deletes the message and an `||` arm deletes
  the exit code; together they make a MISSING subject and a CLEAN subject print
  the same characters. A counter reads the same way: no files and no matches are
  both zero, and zero is what a clean tree prints.

  This does not say the gates are wrong. It says a reader cannot tell, from the
  output, which of the two happened -- and the column above says which subjects
  are absent RIGHT NOW, where the question is no longer hypothetical.

  `[ -f X ]` is listed apart because it is often exactly right: a step that
  legitimately has nothing to do should not fail. What separates it from the
  defect is whether the output NAMES what it read.
